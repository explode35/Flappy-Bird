Send this after the deposit is paid and before the recording session. It
does two things: sets up the recording to actually contain a teachable
framework, and gives you the metadata the pipeline needs (client name,
program length, audience).

## 2. Recording prep (send this section directly to whoever is recording)

You're about to record a 45-minute voice memo. No slides, no script — just
you talking through how you actually do the work. A few prompts to keep you
moving if you get stuck:

1. **The thesis.** If you had to boil down your entire approach into one
   sentence — the thing that, if someone got it, everything else would
   click into place — what is it?
2. **The concepts.** What are the 3-6 core ideas or mental models you use
   that someone outside your head would need to learn? For each, why does
   it matter, and what's a real example?
3. **The process.** Walk through your work step by step, start to finish.
   For each step: what goes in, what comes out, and what mistake do you
   see people make at that step most often?
4. **The heuristics.** What are your rules of thumb — the judgment calls
   you make automatically that a new person would get wrong at first?
5. **The vocabulary.** Are there terms you or your team use that an
   outsider wouldn't know? Define them as you go.
6. **The traps.** What's the mistake that, if a new hire made it, would do
   real damage — and how would you catch it early?

Don't worry about being organized or repeating yourself — ramble if you
need to. The pipeline is built to extract structure from messy, spoken
explanation; it is not built to reward a polished script.
