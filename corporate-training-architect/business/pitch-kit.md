# Pitch Kit — The Corporate Training Architect

Who this is for: solo consultants and boutique agency principals (the
"expert" persona), and HR/L&D leads at mid-size companies who are onboarding
new hires without a real curriculum. Both hold undocumented institutional
knowledge; neither has time to write a training program.

---

## Cold email

**Subject:** Your best employee's brain, in a curriculum, by Friday

Hi {first_name},

Quick question: if your best {role} left tomorrow, could a new hire read
anything and be 80% as good in month one?

For most teams the answer is no — the real expertise lives in one person's
head, and "training" is a Slack channel and tribal knowledge.

I run a service called The Corporate Training Architect. You record one
45-minute voice memo walking through how you actually do the work — your
frameworks, your judgment calls, the mistakes you watch for. I turn that
single recording into a complete 18-week onboarding program: weekly lesson
plans, hands-on activities, and comprehension tests that prove someone
actually learned it — not just sat through it.

No workshops, no multi-week "knowledge capture" project. One recording in,
a shelf-ready curriculum out.

Worth 15 minutes to see a sample built from a real framework?

{your_name}

## The 60-second verbal pitch

"Every expert I talk to has a system in their head that took them a decade
to build, and zero of it is written down anywhere a new hire could learn
from. Writing a real training program from scratch is a 40-hour project
nobody has time for.

So here's what I do: you talk for 45 minutes into your phone, just
explaining how you actually work — no prep, no slides. I run that
recording through a pipeline that extracts your framework, structures it
into an 18-week curriculum, and writes out a complete lesson plan for every
single week — with the hands-on activity, the teaching content, and a test
at the end of each module that actually checks whether someone learned it.

You get back a professional training program that would normally take an
instructional designer a month to build. You spent 45 minutes."

## Discovery call script

1. **Qualify.** "Walk me through how training happens today — is there
   anything written down, or does it live in someone's head?" You want to
   hear: nothing written, high dependency on 1-2 people, new-hire ramp time
   is a known pain point.
2. **Confirm there's a real framework to extract.** "If I asked you to
   explain, start to finish, how you approach {their core process}, could
   you talk for 45 minutes without repeating yourself?" If yes, they're a
   great fit. If they seem unsure they have a "framework" at all, reassure
   them — most experts don't realize how structured their thinking already
   is until they hear it read back to them (this is itself worth saying;
   it's true and it's reassuring).
3. **Set expectations on the deliverable.** Walk through the sample
   package (`sample/` folder run through the pipeline) so they see the
   actual format before buying.
4. **Handle the "will AI botch my expertise" objection** (see below).
5. **Close on the intake questionnaire + recording date**, not a verbal
   yes — get the calendar hold for the 45-minute recording session.

### Objection handling

**"Will an AI actually capture my expertise correctly?"**
"The pipeline doesn't invent anything — it extracts and structures what you
actually say, and it explicitly flags anything ambiguous or missing as a
'gap to clarify' rather than guessing. You'll see that gap list and get to
fill it in before anything ships. I also do a manual review pass on every
deliverable before you see it."

**"Why not just hire an instructional designer?"**
"You can — for $15-30K and 6-8 weeks, mostly spent interviewing you to
extract the same knowledge this pipeline extracts from one recording. This
gets you 90% of the way there in days, at a fraction of the cost, and
leaves budget for a designer to do the last-mile polish if you want it."

**"What if my process doesn't fit neatly into 18 weeks?"**
"18 weeks is the default, not a requirement — I can scope it to whatever
ramp time actually fits your role, from 6 weeks for a narrow function up to
26 for something that takes a full onboarding year. We set that in the
intake call."
